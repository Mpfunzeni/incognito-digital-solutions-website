A website project goes much more smoothly when the important decisions are made before design starts. This checklist walks through the questions we ask every client, so you can arrive at your first meeting prepared.

## 1. Decide what the website must achieve

Start with the outcome, not the design. Most small business websites have one or two main jobs:

- Generating enquiries or quote requests
- Taking bookings or orders
- Building credibility so people who hear about you can check you out
- Answering common customer questions so your team spends less time on the phone

Write your top goal down. It will guide every other decision, including which pages you need and what the main button on each page should say.

## 2. Know who you are speaking to

Describe your ideal customer in a few sentences. What problem are they trying to solve? What would make them choose you over a competitor? What do they need to know before they contact you?

Your website copy should answer those questions directly, in the language your customers use.

## 3. Plan your pages

A typical small business website includes:

- **Home**: a clear summary of what you do, who you help and how to get in touch
- **Services**: ideally one page per main service, so each can explain the details and be found in search
- **About**: who you are and why customers can trust you
- **Contact**: phone, email, a form and, if relevant, your service area
- **Privacy Policy**: required if you collect personal information through forms

Add a blog or resources section if you are willing to publish useful articles regularly.

## 4. Gather your content early

Content is the most common cause of delays. Before the build starts, collect:

- Your logo in high resolution (ideally a vector file)
- Service descriptions, even rough notes
- Photos of your work, team or premises, if you have them
- Genuine testimonials, with the customer's permission

## 5. Sort out your domain, hosting and email

Check who owns your domain name and that it is registered in your business's name. If you do not have a domain yet, choose one that is short, easy to spell and matches your business name. A `.co.za` domain signals clearly that you are a South African business.

## 6. Make the next step obvious

Every page should end with a clear call to action, such as "Get a Quote" or "Call Us". Make phone numbers clickable on mobile, keep forms short, and confirm what happens after someone submits an enquiry.

## 7. Plan for after launch

A website needs updates, backups and occasional content changes. Decide who will handle this before launch, whether that is someone in your team or a maintenance plan.
